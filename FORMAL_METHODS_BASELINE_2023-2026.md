| **[S+]** | Search-confirmed **and** independently corroborated by a second, different search result (e.g. DOI page + proceedings page, or arXiv API record of the same work). |

Conventions and limits, stated plainly:

1. **Nothing here is invented.** Where a work is famous but I could not surface a primary record in this session, it is listed in §10 "Could not verify" rather than cited.
2. **Numbers** are quoted only when they appeared in a page I fetched ([F]) or in a search snippet that reproduced the number; otherwise the claim cell says only what the work establishes.
3. **Paywalled standards** (ISO, IEC, RTCA, IEEE, DO-178C, UL 4600) are cited via their **official catalogue/publisher page**. The normative text itself was not fetched; treat clause-level claims as "not verified in this session".
4. **ACM DL, IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, Springer and doi.org landing pages were blocked** in this environment (HTTP 403 / cross-origin redirect). For those, the artefact is tagged [S]; DOIs are given as `https://doi.org/<doi>` only where the DOI string itself appeared in a search result or an arXiv API record.
5. **Dates**: current date is 2026-09-30, so 2025–2026 venues (FSE 2025, ICSE 2026, TOSEM 2026, ASE 2025, ISSTA 2025) are real and included. Preprint year ≠ venue year in some rows; both are given where known.
6. This file deliberately excludes the LLM-**test-generation-tooling** cluster already covered in FRONTIER_BASELINE_LLM_UNIT_TEST_GENERATION_2023-2026.md (P1-9 修正：原引用指向仓库中不存在的文件，断链；该职能实际由前沿测试生成基线文档承担); overlap is limited to the neuro-symbolic and oracle rows that the present brief explicitly requires.

## 8. Consensus vs. contested vs. assumption

### Consensus (safe to assert as established)
1. **Test coverage is a weak proxy for fault detection.** Inozemtseva & Holmes (ICSE 2014) is the load-bearing citation; it is independently supported by mutation-vs-coverage industrial practice (Petrović & Ivanković, ICSE-SEIP 2018) and by Shamshiri et al. (ASE 2015) showing generated tests find few real faults. Treat "we raised coverage" as a *necessary but insufficient* claim.
2. **LLMs generate tests cheaply but with wrong oracles.** ChatTester quantifies it (+34.3% compilable, +18.7% correct-assertion after repair; assertions are the dominant failure), MuTAP attacks it with mutation feedback (up to +28% faulty snippets detected), TOGLL attacks it by fine-tuning oracle generation (3.8× correct assertions). Three independent methods converging on the same defect class is consensus, not a single paper.
3. **Filtering gates are the industrial adoption mechanism.** Meta's TestGen-LLM (75%/57%/25% filters; 73% engineer acceptance) is the reference point: generation is not the contribution; the guarantee pipeline is.
4. **Contracts/specs are executable oracles when written in a checkable language** (ACSL/e-ACSL, JML, Dafny, icontract/deal/CrossHair, Kani harnesses). This is definitional rather than empirical.
5. **Symbolic/concolic execution is a test-input generator, bounded in practice.** KLEE → SymCC → Rampo lineage; CBMC documents loop-bound-based practice rather than decidability.
6. **SMT solvers are the shared substrate across spec languages.** Dafny/Verus/Creusot/Why3/Boogie all reduce to SMT (Z3/cvc5) — which is also why they inherit the same float/string/quantifier weaknesses.
7. **MC/DC has measurable, structure-dependent value.** Whalen et al.'s TOSEM study finds MC/DC-adequate suites can expose faults that decision coverage misses, while explicitly refusing a universal superiority claim.
8. **Test smells are detectable and prevalent in LLM-generated suites** (Assertion Roulette, Magic Number Test), though detector reliability is contested (see below).

### Contested (cite both sides; do not assert one winner)
1. **"LLM tests are as good as human tests."** TestGen-LLM/Meta (acceptance-based filters, production deployment) and SWT-Bench (generated tests *double* SWE-Agent precision) support the optimistic reading; ChatTester, the TOGLL oracle study, test-smell diffusion (TOSEM 2026), and "Do LLMs Generate Useful Test Oracles?" with an *unbiased dataset* undercut it. Contested axis: **benchmark bias vs. deployment evidence**.
2. **Coverage vs. mutation as the acceptance metric.** MuTAP and CoverUp/Ryan et al. disagree implicitly: CoverUp optimises coverage and wins on coverage; MuTAP optimises mutants and wins on bug detection. Reporting either alone invites reviewer attack.
3. **MC/DC adequacy.** Its defenders (Whalen et al.; DO-178C practice) vs. critiques that MC/DC is structure-sensitive, expensive, and not a fault-detection guarantee; the "coverage is not adequacy" line is explicit in Gay et al. (TSE 2015).
4. **Equivalent mutants.** Papadakis et al. (TSE 2014) show second-order mutation and heuristics only partially solve it; the 2025 ML-based review shows the problem is still open. Anyone quoting a mutation score must address this.
5. **LLM-as-judge for test validity.** MT-Bench judge study documents position/verbiage/self-preference bias; the 2025 test-coverage judge study claims usable accuracy at lower cost. Unresolved: judge reliability under distribution shift.
6. **Guarantees from bounded verification.** Kani/CBMC give *bounded* proofs; "verified" in a README is contested unless the bound and the harness are stated. Same issue for invariant generation without loop unrolling (ASE 2024).
7. **Standards coverage obligations under AI.** ISO 26262/IEC 61508 predate ML-based components; ISO/PAS 8800:2024 and ISO/IEC TR 5469:2024 are the attempted fixes and are still being absorbed. Cite them as *emerging*, not settled.
8. **Equivalent-mutant-free mutation scores / sniff-test adequacy** — and the meta-question of whether mutation testing itself is cost-effective at scale; Petrović & Ivanković's answer (only with selection) is not universal.

### Assumptions (state them explicitly if you rely on them)
1. **"Coverage computed the same way is comparable across tools."** Python (coverage.py), JVM (JaCoCo), and LLVM (SanitizerCoverage) instrument different units; cross-paper coverage comparisons are assumption-laden.
2. **"A passing generated test means the code is correct."** Only under the (usually false) assumption that the oracle is correct and the input distribution is representative — the oracle-problem assumption.
3. **"The benchmark's golden tests are valid."** EvalPlus exists because this assumption failed (HumanEval/MBPP pass@k collapsed under stronger tests); the evalplus issue #4 trail shows it still fails occasionally.
4. **"Specification = intent."** Clover's contribution is precisely that spec/proof/code can be mutually consistent yet wrong; LLM-generated specs inherit the same risk.
5. **"Mutation score ≈ fault detection."** Depends on mutant operators resembling real faults (the Just et al. lineage); unstated when teams quote MS as a quality gate.
6. **"Formal verification is too expensive to apply broadly."** Repeated as folklore (CompCert's proofs are ~8× the code, per the Dafny blog); the *rate of change* under LLM assistance (15.7% → 50.6% annotation success) is the variable that determines whether the assumption still holds.
7. **"Equivalent mutants are a rounding error."** No verified evidence supports a small constant rate; assume it is unknown per project.
8. **"Standards compliance implies adequacy."** DO-178C/ISO 26262/IEC 61508 certify *process and evidence*, not freedom from defects; SOTIF/UL 4600/ISO 8800 exist because that gap is real.

## 9. Table stakes a 2026 reviewer expects

If your paper claims logic- or specification-driven test generation, a 2026 reviewer will check for these. Missing items are rejection risks, not stylistic gaps.

**A. Baselines (must appear)**
1. An **SBST baseline**: EvoSuite (Java) or Pynguin (Python) — not optional.
2. A **coverage-feedback LLM baseline**: CoverUp (FSE 2025) for Python, or SymPrompt/Code-Aware Prompting (FSE 2024) for path-guided prompting. Claiming "logic-driven" novelty without a SymPrompt-style path-constraint ablation is the single most common gap.
3. A **repair-loop baseline**: ChatUniTest (generation–validation–repair) or ChatTester (iterative refinement).
4. A **mutation-guided baseline** if you claim fault detection: MuTAP or an industrial mutation-guided setup.
5. **Industrial filter comparison**: state your analogue of TestGen-LLM's build/reliable/coverage-improvement gates.

**B. Task framing and benchmarks**
6. **Method-level vs class-level vs repository-level** must be declared; TestBench (class-level) and SWT-Bench (issue-level) are not interchangeable.
7. For issue→test tasks: **SWT-Bench** (and say whether you used the Lite/Verified subset) or **TDD-Bench Verified** (test-before-fix, no leakage).
8. For code correctness claims: **EvalPlus** (HumanEval+/MBPP+) rather than plain HumanEval/MBPP.
9. **Data-leakage control**: contamination checks for benchmark instances present in pretraining (cf. miniF2F-Lean Revisited for the proving side).

**C. Metrics that survive review**
10. Report **both** coverage and a fault-sensitive metric (mutation score or real-fault detection); never coverage alone (Inozemtseva & Holmes; Shamshiri et al.).
11. Report **correct-assertion rate / oracle correctness** separately from compilability (ChatTester's protocol).
12. Report **false-positive rate** for the tests you generate (tests that fail on correct code) — the LLM-oracle literature makes this mandatory.
13. Report **test-smell prevalence** (Assertion Roulette, Magic Number Test) if you claim maintainability — and use at least two detectors because detectors disagree (Pecorelli et al.).
14. If you use an LLM judge, report **judge-vs-ground-truth agreement** and control for position/verbosity bias (MT-Bench).
15. Report **cost and token budget** per accepted test (the "pass@T / cost per verified artifact" convention introduced by VerMCTS-style work).

**D. Specification and formal-method hygiene**
16. State the **specification language** (JML/ACSL/Dafny/TLA+/Alloy/contracts) and whether specs are human-written, mined (Daikon/LLM), or LLM-generated.
17. If you generate specs, report **spec correctness**, not just usefulness — Clover's code/spec/proof consistency loop is the expected protocol.
18. State **solver and version** (Z3/cvc5), **timeouts**, and what happens on `unknown` — `unknown` is the dominant real-world SMT outcome and is routinely hidden.
19. For bounded tools (CBMC/Kani), state the **unwinding bound** and translate results as "no counterexample within bound k", never "verified".
20. For float/string-heavy code, acknowledge the **known theory limitations** rather than reporting a single aggregate solve rate.

**E. Empirical-validity mechanics**
21. **Pre-register or justify** the unit of analysis; per-module medians and overall aggregates differ (CoverUp reports both, and they differ).
22. **Multiple models** (at least one open-weight) and **prompt-sensitivity analysis**; single-model, single-prompt results are treated as anecdotal in 2025–2026.
23. **Non-determinism control**: fixed seeds/temperature, and repeat runs with variance reported.
24. **Flakiness and timeout handling** in the harness (ICSE 2024 SAP HANA study), including retry policy, since generated tests are flakiness-prone.
25. **Artifact availability**: code + benchmark subsets + raw logs; SWT-Bench/EvalPlus set the norm.

**F. Standards and regulation literacy (if you claim industrial relevance)**
26. Map your evidence to **ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-4** technique names and to **ISO/IEC 25010:2023** quality characteristics (note: 2023 edition, nine characteristics).
27. If you touch safety-critical domains, name the applicable framework precisely: **DO-178C + DO-333** (airborne), **ISO 26262-6 + ISO/PAS 21448 + ISO/PAS 8800:2024** (automotive), **IEC 61508-3 / EN 50128** (industrial/rail), **UL 4600 + IEEE 7009-2024** (autonomy/fail-safe).
28. For AI components, align claims with **EU AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689)** high-risk testing duties, **NIST AI RMF + AI 600-1**, **ISO/IEC 42001**, and **ISO/IEC TR 5469:2024** — and be explicit about which are voluntary vs. legally binding.
